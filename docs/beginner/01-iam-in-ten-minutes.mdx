Identity and Access Management (IAM) answers two questions for every request: who is this, and what are they allowed to do.

- **Identity:** a record of a person, a machine or an AI agent, with attributes (email, name) and credentials (password, passkey, client secret).
- **Authentication (authn):** proving who you are. Ex: password, one-time code (OTP), passkey, signing in with Google.
- **Authorization (authz):** deciding what an authenticated identity can do. Ex: a role that grants `orders:read`.
- **Identity provider (IdP):** the system that does authentication and issues proof of it. ThunderID is an IdP. Google or another company's IdP can be an upstream IdP that ThunderID trusts (federation).

## OAuth 2.0 and OpenID Connect

Most of ThunderID's external surface is these two standards.

- **OAuth 2.0** ([RFC 6749](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc6749)) is about delegated access. A **client** (your app) gets an **access token** from an **authorization server** (ThunderID) and presents it to a **resource server** (your API).
- **OpenID Connect (OIDC)** sits on top of OAuth 2.0 and adds sign-in. The client also gets an **ID token** that says who the user is.
- A **grant type** is a way to get a token. The common ones: `authorization_code` (a user signs in through a browser redirect), `client_credentials` (a service gets a token for itself), `refresh_token` (get a new access token without signing in again).
- **PKCE** ([RFC 7636](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc7636)) protects the authorization code from being stolen and replayed. Browser and mobile apps always use it.

## Tokens

- **Access token:** sent to APIs. Carries `scope` (what it's allowed to do) and `aud` (which API it's for).
- **ID token:** read by the client. Carries `sub` (the user's identifier) and profile claims.
- **Refresh token:** used only at the token endpoint to get new tokens.
- ThunderID issues tokens as signed **JWTs** ([RFC 7519](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc7519)). Anyone can verify the signature with the public keys at the JWKS endpoint. Paste one into a JWT decoder during the labs to see the claims.
